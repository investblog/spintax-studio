Spintax Studio is an offline desktop application. It does not collect, transmit or store personal data.

What we collect
Nothing. There is no account to create, no sign-in, no licence check, no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising. We do not know that you have installed the application, and we have no way to find out from it.

What leaves your computer
Nothing the application sends. The editor, validation, rendering, variant generator, export and built-in help work without a network connection.

There are two links in the window, and a link is not a request. The spintax.net mark at the bottom of the tool rail and the 301.st mark at the right end of the status bar each do one thing when you click them: they ask Windows to open that address. Windows hands it to whatever browser you use, and the browser is what then visits the site, under its own privacy terms and that site's, exactly as if you had typed the address yourself. Spintax Studio is not part of that exchange, sends nothing into it and makes no network request of its own. Nothing is opened unless you click.

What is stored, and where
Your settings, including interface language, theme, editor font and panel widths, are written to a text file in your user profile (%LOCALAPPDATA%). Installed from the Microsoft Store, that file lives inside the application's package container. It contains preferences only: no documents, personal details or device identifiers.

Your documents stay where you put them. Templates, template sets and exported variants are ordinary files in folders you choose. The application reads and writes them only when you ask it to and never copies them elsewhere.

Removing the application removes its settings with it.

Children
The application collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy
A later version may add optional AI assistance that sends text you choose to a provider you configure with your own key. That is not in this version. This policy will be updated and republished before any version that does so.
